Point.

One design concept for the lead-gen redesign: everywhere on the site, separate things resolve into one calm point of contact — because that's what STS already sells, and what the mark on your own logo already draws.

Ambient sketch — the convergence gesture this whole system is built around.

01Concept

Relief, not firepower.

The locked copy is blunt about the problem: a business owner stuck refereeing between a host and a developer who each blame the other, paying several people to maintain one website. STS's pitch isn't "we're more technical than them" — it's "stop juggling, there's one number to call now." That's a feeling of relief, not a demonstration of skill, and the visual system has to lead with the former.

That idea turns out to already be sitting in the brand mark.

What the logo already says

Two chevrons taper inward and meet the oval at its center — a convergence gesture, already drawn, already approved, already yours. Purple and olive green are not new choices for this redesign; they are inherited directly from this mark and stay fixed.

Gold is the one real addition — already present informally in the illustrations, promoted here into an intentional third role. See 02 — Color.

02Color

Keep what's earned its place.

Purple and green stay exactly as they are — 88 combined uses across the site and, more importantly, the logo. What changes is discipline: three near-identical greens in the current codebase collapse into one documented pair, and gold stops being an accident twelve components independently reached for and becomes a named role — warmth and reassurance, used for the one offer (the Growth Bundle) the copy deliberately keeps out of every comparison table.

06Illustration

The signature of the whole redesign.

Three components already do this right — Partnership, ProcessJourney, WhatEStoreIncludes — while WhyMe and a handful of leftover PNG icons still run the older icon-row pattern. This is that language, named, so it can finish rolling out everywhere instead of stopping at three components.

  • The convergence gesture. Every signature scene stages some version of separate things becoming one — two paths, a hand and a plant, light gathering to a point. Not literal every time; always present.
  • Soft rounded flat vector, pastel scene-tint panels, small looped ambient motion — sway, bob, twinkle, pulse. Already built, already respects reduced motion. Kept exactly as-is.
  • Icon rows retire completely. Scenes for storytelling moments, small utility icons only for pure UI (a WhatsApp glyph, a form checkmark) — never mixed in the same section.
  • Variants are welcome, the way Partnership already offers tree/lighthouse — different pages showing the same idea differently is the goal, not an inconsistency to fix.

Concept sketch, not final art — the exact composition each scene takes is Phase 2 work. This is the grammar those scenes will share.

Style reference: Partnership's tree scene, ProcessJourney's storyboard panels.

07Motion

Ambient, not decorative.

No new dependency — the site's existing CSS-native vocabulary (fade/slide entrances, hover lifts, ambient sway/bob/twinkle/pulse, full prefers-reduced-motion support) is already right and extends to every new scene. One orchestrated moment per page beats scattered effects everywhere — the hero sketch above and the scene in section 06 are that vocabulary, not a new one.
